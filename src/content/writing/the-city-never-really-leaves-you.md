so i was in edinburgh recently, and i need to talk about it, because walking through that city did something to me that no city has done before.

![a city street lined with tall buildings and flags](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1691201200746-9a95b64a3436?crop=entropy&cs=tinysrgb&fit=max&fm=jpg&ixid=M3wzMDAzMzh8MHwxfHNlYXJjaHwyN3x8ZWRpbmJ1cmdofGVufDB8fHx8MTc4ODM2OTQwOXww&ixlib=rb-4.1.0&q=80&w=1080)

*Photo by [Joss Broward](https://unsplash.com/@jossbroward) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com)*

the whole place is stunning. creative, joyful, almost lifted, like the streets themselves are in a good mood. i’ve never felt energy like that from a place. and edinburgh is literally known as a city of literature, which started making a lot of sense to me by like day two.

at some point i ended up at the elephant house, one of the cafés where jk rowling wrote the early harry potter books. and the detail that got me is that from the back of the café, through the window, you can see edinburgh castle. she was sitting in a café, broke, looking at an actual castle, writing a story about a castle. and i just sat there thinking: yeah. of course this city produced that.

### cities produce things

that’s the thought i couldn’t shake. the greatest things humanity makes seem to come out of specific places. edinburgh produces literature. san francisco produces startups. and it’s not that talented people only exist in those cities, it’s that something in the environment, the energy, the density of people doing the same kind of dreaming, makes the work more possible there.

which forced me to ask the uncomfortable version of the question: what did my city produce?

### what my city installed

i grew up in singapore. and singapore produces some of the most focused, disciplined, relentlessly competitive people you will ever meet. everything there was calculating. everything was a ranking. and i have to be honest with you: i’m a product of that. the drive i have, the way i lock onto goals, some of that machinery was installed before i had any say in it.

for a long time my theory was simple: the city you’re raised in builds your core, and the cities you live in later can only renovate the surface. and the surface stuff definitely happened. when i moved to canada, my mannerisms changed completely. in singapore people are reserved, and the culture can be pretty cold day to day. here, people small talk with strangers and are weirdly, wonderfully open to helping you. i love it so much that i became it.

but i assumed that was the extent of it. new coat of paint, same core underneath.

### except the core moved too

here’s what actually broke my theory. it’s not just my mannerisms that changed in canada. the *nature* of my drive changed.

the drive singapore installed was zero-sum. get to the top, and the top is a fixed number of seats, so everyone around you is competition. someone else winning meant something was taken from you. it’s an elitist kind of hunger, and it’s exhausting, and for a long time i thought that hunger simply *was* my drive.

the drive i see around me in canada is different. people lock onto their own mission and run at it, and they respect everyone at every level while doing it. your friend succeeding isn’t a threat, it’s just good news. and slowly, without me scheduling it, my drive shifted to run on that instead. i’m genuinely happier seeing my friends succeed now than i used to be. it’s kind of sad to admit there was a “used to be,” but it’s true, and i think honesty is the whole point of this essay.

### the outsider thing

which brings me back to rowling, because here’s the part i find funny: she’s not from edinburgh. she grew up in the english countryside, spent time working in london, and arrived in edinburgh in her twenties with an unfinished manuscript and not much else. the city of literature didn’t produce her. she walked into it and absorbed it.

and tech works exactly the same way. so many of the defining startups of our era weren’t founded by people born anywhere near san francisco. patrick and john collison grew up in rural ireland, and it was going to the bay area, plugging into that environment, going through y combinator, that turned their ideas into stripe. the city didn’t make them. they arrived as outsiders, absorbed what that environment had, and produced exactly the kind of thing that environment produces.

and maybe outsiders don’t just absorb a city’s energy. maybe they feel it at *full strength*, precisely because it’s not their default. someone born in edinburgh walks past that castle every day and stops seeing it. someone raised on sf ambition might not even notice the water they swim in. but walk in from the outside, from a different default, and the contrast hits you like weather. i felt edinburgh so intensely *because* i wasn’t made there. i felt canada’s version of drive so clearly *because* singapore gave me something to compare it against.

### where i landed

so here’s my revised conclusion. the city you’re raised in absolutely writes your defaults, your core, the machinery underneath. that part of my old theory holds. but the core isn’t permanent. it can be dismantled and reshaped, and the way it happens is that injection of energy you get by standing in a different city as an outsider, feeling what it runs on, and letting some of it in.

it’s not automatic though. i think it takes awareness. when i realized which parts of the singapore mindset were harming me, i didn’t passively grow out of them. i noticed them, named them, and worked them out of myself on purpose, with canada’s energy as the replacement part.

you can leave the city, but the city never really leaves you. unless you walk into another one, feel what it has to offer, and rewrite yourself on purpose.
