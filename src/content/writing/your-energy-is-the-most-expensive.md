so i used to spend a genuinely embarrassing amount of time scrolling instagram. and not even good instagram. i’m talking brain rot. the most fried, meaningless, AI slop content imaginable, and i found it hilarious.

and honestly? i get why i was doing it. i was in IB at the time, and if you know, you know. i was stressed constantly, and the scroll was the one place where nothing was asked of me. so without really noticing, i started treating brain rot as, like, the food for my soul. that was the nourishment i was giving myself at the end of every day.

it took finishing my exams, going on vacation, and properly detaching from everything to see it clearly: where we direct our energy matters so much, because our energy is precious and limited. and i was pouring mine into content that was engineered to catch it, not content that deserved it.

### everything wants your attention

here’s the thing i keep coming back to. the news, advertisements, social media, all of it is competing for exactly one resource: your attention. that’s the whole game. and whatever wins your attention gets to decide what narratives you’re exposed to, over and over, until they quietly become part of how you see the world.

and i think that’s the real frame for all of this. the narratives we consume either empower us or disempower us. there isn’t really a neutral option.

content that teaches you something, or makes you feel genuinely good, or helps you build a worldview you actually believe in? that’s empowering. it compounds. but the cheap stuff, the slop, the stuff made purely for the dopamine hit? it feels good in the moment, and then you step away and realize you don’t feel good at all about where that hour went. and over time i think it does something sneakier: it disempowers you, because on some level you start to believe that this meaningless content is what you’re worthy of. that this is your level. if that makes sense.

for knowledge, most of us are covered. we have school, work, whatever we’re building. but food for the *soul* is the thing nobody hands you a curriculum for, and it’s the thing the algorithm is happiest to replace with junk.

### what i changed

i started reading classics, and lowkey found a love for literature i didn’t know i had. i’ve been reading Jane Austen, and it’s so interesting to watch her build out the societal norms and conventions of her world and show how deeply they shape people.

and here’s the part that surprised me: it changed how i watch everything else too. i’ve been watching Marvel movies lately, which, i know, does not immediately scream “meaningful content.” but i’ve been watching them through this lens from Northrop Frye’s writing about literature, noticing the conventions of the superhero story, the patterns, how they rhyme with stuff that’s centuries old. the same movie i would’ve half-watched while scrolling became genuinely interesting, because i brought something to it. meaningful consumption is honestly less about *what* you watch and more about the lens you bring.

even my scrolling changed. i still scroll, i’m not going to pretend i became a monk. but now it’s mostly substack, reading people’s thoughtful little epiphanies and reflections, and those insights actually make me feel good in a way that lasts past the scroll.

and here’s the kicker: i think that feeling is what i was looking for the entire time. even deep in my brain rot era, i was seeking *something* from the scroll. some sense of meaning, or connection, or a thought worth keeping. i was just looking for it in a place that was structurally incapable of providing it.

### so, the point

you can’t always control what content exists, or how aggressively it’s pushed at you. but you can be intentional about what you let in. and i think the question worth asking about anything you’re consuming is really simple: is this narrative empowering me or disempowering me? is this making me more of who i want to be, or quietly convincing me to want less?

your energy is the food you feed your soul. the algorithm is very happy to cater, buttt… maybe don’t let it pick the menu.
