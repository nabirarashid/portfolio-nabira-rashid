so recently i came across a video telling me that if i host my projects on Vercel instead of Cloudflare, i’m not gonna make it. NGMI, as they say. not gonna make it, because of a *deployment platform.*

and i want to be clear, this isn’t about that one video. this has just become the default register of tech content lately, and i’ve been sitting on this for a while, so here we go.

here’s the thing that actually bothers me: the video was *informative.* there are real, legitimate reasons to consider Cloudflare over Vercel. pricing, edge network, whatever. genuinely useful information that i would have happily received. but instead of “hey, if you’re on Vercel, here’s why you might consider switching,” the framing was: if you’re doing it this way, you’re not good enough. you’re a bum.

same information. completely different intent. and the intent is the product.

### the pattern

once you see it, you can’t unsee it. someone discovers a niche tool, or a better workflow, or some obscure config trick. and instead of just... sharing it... they arrive at the conclusion that knowing this thing makes them superior. then they get online and inform everyone else that they’re falling behind.

it’s not information sharing, it’s simply status signaling in disguise.

and i get *why* it happens, i really do. the tech space is brutally competitive right now, and everyone in it has this almost innate need to be seen as cracked, as special, as ahead. we can’t tolerate the idea that someone else might be better than us. so when we find a small edge, even something as small as knowing about a tool earlier than everyone else, we cash it in for the cheapest form of superiority available: making someone else feel behind.

### the part that’s personal

i felt this firsthand before i really had words for it. last year, i built an agent that scraped scholarship opportunities off the internet so i’d never miss a deadline, and i made a video about it. some “more technical” people i knew poked fun at it. not “hey, cool start, have you thought about making it *apply* for you too?” just... that it was kind of lame. that i should’ve built something more impressive.

and honestly? the auto-applying version *would* have been cooler. the feedback wasn’t even wrong. but there’s a version of that feedback that builds you and a version that just establishes the pecking order, and they picked the second one. they almost always pick the second one.

i stopped making content for a while after that. which is exactly the problem. this culture doesn’t just bruise egos, it filters out beginners. it decides who gets to keep showing up.

### the ask

we’ve all been the person who didn’t know the thing yet. every single one of us. the senior dev roasting your stack once deployed something embarrassing to production. the person calling your project “lame” once built a to-do app and was proud of it, as they should have been.

everyone is on a different trajectory. everyone works at a different pace. and the people starting out right now, the ones getting told they’re NGMI over a hosting provider, deserve the same space to be new that everyone before them got.

so if you know something someone else doesn’t: congrats, genuinely. now say it like you want them to succeed. “consider Cloudflare, here’s why” costs you nothing. the condescension was never load-bearing.

knowing about a tool three weeks early is not a personality. helping someone catch up? getting there.
