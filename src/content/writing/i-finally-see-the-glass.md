okay so i just got back from new york and san francisco and i am so tired. sleep schedule in pieces, spent money i probably shouldn’t have. and the thing i keep thinking about wasn’t a conversation or a connection or anything i could put on a resume. it was more like a setting got changed somewhere in the back of my head.

let me try to explain it, because i think it’s the most useful thing i’ve figured out this year.

## permission by proximity

[catgpt](https://www.instagram.com/askcatgpt/?hl=en) said something once (catherine goetze, she makes tech content, worth the follow) and it rewired me. the point of going to a school like stanford or mit isn’t really the network. the network is a factor, sure. but the actual thing is that you spend four years surrounded by people doing slightly absurd things, and after a while your brain quietly reclassifies those things. they move from “what impressive people do” to “what people do.” and then eventually to “what i could do.”

you don’t get smarter by proximity. you get permission by proximity.

for context, i’m going to waterloo. i got rejected from the ivies, from stanford, from mit, which as a canadian applicant is a pretty normal outcome and also gutted me in a way i’ve never really written about.

## okay so the fish tank thing

the way i picture it: my sense of what’s possible for me is a fish tank, and i’m the fish. before this summer, the walls of that tank were built out of what waterloo students have done, which, to be clear, is not a small tank. people from my school start yc companies, they land the internships everyone posts about, the bar is genuinely high. but it was a specific shape, and i had never once had a thought about my own life that came from outside it.

here’s what i keep turning over though. the walls of a fish tank are glass, and you don’t see glass. the fish doesn’t swim up to the edge and think “ah, a constraint.” it just never goes there, because as far as it can tell there’s nothing over there to go to. that’s the whole trick of this kind of ceiling. it doesn’t feel like a limit, it feels like the edge of what exists.

and that’s the part that should scare you a little. you are not walking around feeling capped. you’re walking around feeling like you’ve got a realistic sense of yourself.

## the two rooms

this summer i ended up in two rooms. a program in new york run by a quant firm, and a startup school type event in san francisco, both full of people already doing real things. builders, researchers, people with actual users. the new york one especially was a room full of very sharp women, and i’m still unpacking what that did to me. i was one of the only waterloo people there. everyone else was ivy or stanford or mit.

i walked in expecting to feel small. instead something in the back of my head quietly resized.

the tank went up like five times, not because anyone said anything profound, just because i was in it. someone had read my application and decided i belonged in a chair next to those people. and once you’re actually sitting in the chair, the old ceiling gets really hard to believe in.

so that’s the thing i want to make the whole point of this: the rooms do the work. not the credential, not the accomplishment. the room.

## then i got home, and i almost got the mechanism wrong

then something changed that i want to be honest about, because my first read of it was flattering and probably wrong.

there are people i requested on instagram months ago, fairly well known in this space, who never accepted. nothing, ever. after the trip i requested a few people at roughly that same level, and not only did they accept, some followed me back.

and my first theory was very satisfying and very main character of me. i changed how i see myself, i put that energy out into the world, the world responded in kind. beautiful. extremely postable.

wait. no.

that’s the flattering half. because the other thing that happened is that i became legible. i have things on my profile that did not exist in june. i have mutuals with those people. i’m in the photos from those events. someone opening my profile today is looking at a genuinely different pile of evidence than someone who opened it in may. that’s not energy. that’s a signal, and it’s one they can verify in about four seconds.

but here’s why i’m telling you this, and it’s not to talk myself out of the psychology. it’s that i had the mechanism backwards, and the real one is better.

the psychology didn’t reach out into the world and change how people saw me. the psychology made me apply. it made me hit submit on things i would have quietly decided weren’t for me six months ago. the applying put me in the rooms. the rooms made me legible. and the legibility is what other people actually responded to.

so it goes: what you think is possible decides what you apply to. what you apply to decides which rooms you get into. the rooms decide what you think is possible next. and around it goes.

that’s the loop. and the only place you can put your hands on it is the first step, which is entirely in your head.

## so about the cards

i’ve been saying to friends that life is just psychology, and i want to be careful with that, because said flatly it’s the kind of line that turns into exactly the sort of post i started writing to complain about.

cards are real. i know they’re real. where you were born, what your family has, what your school gives you access to, whether anyone in your life ever told you a certain kind of future was for people like you. those aren’t psychological, those are conditions, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something.

but the cards aren’t the game. the cards are the difficulty setting. they decide how many rooms there are, how far away they are, how much it costs you to get to one. what they don’t decide is whether you try the door. and i think most people, especially the women i’ve met in this industry, are stopped way earlier than their cards actually stop them. they’re stopped at a wall they built themselves out of one bad season.

so there are two ceilings. one is real, made of actual constraints, and it isn’t going anywhere. the other is the glass one, the tank wall, the one you’re carrying around in your head. for most of us the glass one sits way lower than the real one. and the gap between them is the entire game. it’s the only part that’s yours, and it’s also bigger than you think.

## back to waterloo

which is the funny part, because my lowest glass wall was waterloo itself.

i’ve spent a year describing my school as the step below, the consolation card. and this summer showed me that ceiling was never measuring anything real. waterloo puts people in those exact rooms. i was in the room. the room did not check. the ceiling wasn’t made of waterloo, it was made of what i decided waterloo meant. i built it during a bad admissions season and then just left it there.

i’ve written before about what your environment installs in you without asking permission. this is the same thing, except i installed it myself. which is worse. and also better, because it means i can take it back out.

## so what do you actually do

nothing here is about having more. i didn’t get smarter in one week and i didn’t come home with better cards. i have the exact same brain and the exact same resume i left with, plus some photos.

the only thing that changed is what i think is available to me, and that changed because i physically went and stood somewhere that made the old estimate impossible to keep believing.

so that’s the practice, and i think it’s the whole thing. keep deliberately putting yourself in rooms that are slightly beyond what you think you’re qualified for. apply to the thing you’d normally scroll past. every room you get into resets your sense of what a normal ambition looks like, and that new sense is what makes you apply to the next one.

if you’re somewhere that feels like it’s capping you, i’d gently ask when you last checked that the walls are where you think they are. go stand in a room you’re not sure you belong in. worst case you were right, and at least now you know where the wall actually is instead of guessing at it from a distance.

and seeing it doesn’t break it, to be clear. the glass is still glass. some of those walls are real and they are staying exactly where they are. but there’s a difference between a wall you can’t see and a wall you can. the one you can’t see feels like the edge of the world. the one you can see is just a wall. it has a location. you can put your hand flat against it, and then you can find out how much of the tank you were never using.

i’m the same fish, in the same water. same brain, same cards, same everything i left with. the only thing that changed size is the tank. and that’s the point, because it’s the only thing that compounds.
